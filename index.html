Robotics · VisionSept – Nov 2026

        
YARC: camera perception for a rail-inspection robot

        
YARC is a start-up building a trolley that rides the rails and inspects them. In a five-person interdisciplinary TU Delft team I owned the camera perception subsystem: recording, dataset, detection models and the live system on the trolley.

        
    
          
  • Three views, three detectors. A top-down HD camera over the rail finds sleepers, fasteners, bolts and clips (YOLO26n). A forward 4K camera reads sleepers and switches (YOLO26s). A 360° camera watches for people and vehicles (COCO-pretrained YOLO26n). The trained models are fine-tuned from COCO weights, because I had dozens of labelled frames, not thousands.
    • 
          
  • Detect components, infer defects. Real defects are too rare to train on. So the models find components and simple rules flag what is missing. One rule, run over 1,529 frames, turned a single known missing-bolt example into 11 more confirmed ones.
    • 
          
  • Recording pipeline on ROS 2. The stock camera driver recorded corrupt frames, so I wrote a node that passes the camera's MJPEG straight through into MCAP bags at about 30 Hz. Frames are then sampled by distance travelled, measured with phase correlation.
    • 
          
  • Evaluated on track it had never seen. The data is split by track, not by frame, so neighbouring frames cannot leak into the test.
    • 
          
  • Live on the trolley. One ROS 2 detector node per camera, always working on the newest frame. All three ran together during a two-day field test in October 2026.
    •

NLPApr – Jun 2026

          
Multilingual hate speech detection

          
How much harder is implicit hate to catch than explicit hate, and does it depend on the language and the kind of model? In a team of four we built an end-to-end pipeline (a YAML-configured CLI for data preparation, synthetic data, training and evaluation). I owned the encoder: XLM-RoBERTa fine-tuned on a 28,970-sample corpus in 7 languages.

          
The encoder drops from 0.85 to 0.53 F1 on implicit hate (p < 0.001), worst in German. Few-shot Aya-23, Llama-3-8B and Mistral-7B showed a gap of at most 0.01 on the same benchmark, and adding explanations to the prompt barely helped.

Research · Published2025 – 2026

          
Simulating a disorder as an adaptive network

          
My bachelor's thesis at VU Amsterdam, now a paper: Understanding Body Dysmorphic Disorder Through Dynamic Network Simulation and Roleplay-Based Visualization. It models one episode in the life of a teenager, Ava, as a network of 37 states on three layers: mental states and behaviours, connections that strengthen with use (Hebbian learning), and a layer that controls how fast that learning happens.

          
In simulation the symptoms escalate fast and almost in sync. Stronger self-control barely changes that, and a friend's well-meant reassurance ends up feeding the loop. I then retold the run as an AI-generated role-play video, so non-modellers can watch the cycle unfold.

          

          
Presented at the Computing Conference 2026 in London and published by Springer Nature in Intelligent Computing, Volume 2. First author, with I.-D. Obada, R. M. Treur, S. C. F. Hendrikse and J. Treur.
